\begin{abstract}
We report on a machine-checked study of the two positivity conjectures for Morin
singularities. The infrastructure computes $\Qd_d$ for $d \le 7$ from the explicit orbit
equations of B\'erczi--Szenes \S7.3, self-checked against every invariant fixed in advance and
agreeing with Rim\'anyi's published tables wherever those reach.
On top of it: Rim\'anyi's conjecture is verified for $A_6$ (first time), the two reductions of
the $A_5$ handoff note are shown to survive at $d = 6$ but to \emph{fail} at $d = 7$ -- while
Rim\'anyi's conjecture itself survives there, so the reduction machinery, not the conjecture, is
what is specific to small $d$ --- the proposed prefix-positivity route is shown to fail already at
$d = 6$, and a notion of \emph{denominator certificate} is
introduced whose successes are proofs and whose failures are theorems. The certificate
machinery reproduces the $d = 4$ strong positivity theorem and Proposition~3 of the handoff
note mechanically.  All coefficients agree with Rim\'anyi's independently computed tables
wherever those reach, including $A_7$. A final section takes up the observation of [S] that
the numerator is determined only up to a residue-null kernel, which scopes the certificate
obstructions above and supplies the one route not already known to be specific to small $d$.

Notation follows \texttt{rimanyi\_positivity.pdf},
\texttt{report.pdf} and \texttt{a5\_weak\_positivity\_handoff.pdf}, cited as [N], [R], [H].
Two further unrefereed reports are cited: \texttt{rimanyi\_positivity\_findings\_summary.pdf}
(29 July 2026) as [S], the primary source for the universal kernel and the $d = 5$ theorem,
and \texttt{a6\_status.pdf} (31 July 2026) as [A6], which imports the latter and extends the
construction to $d = 6$. Where the two overlap we cite [S], which carries the proofs.
\end{abstract}

\section{Setting and notation}

For the Morin singularity $A_d$, the B\'erczi--Szenes formula expresses $\Tp_{A_d}$ as an
iterated residue whose only $d$-dependent input is $\Qd_d = \mdeg_{T_d}(\mathcal{O}_d)$, the
equivariant multidegree of the Borel orbit closure $\mathcal{O}_d = \overline{B_d \cdot \eref}$
inside $\Nhat_d \subset \Hom(\mathbb{C}^d, \Sym^2 \mathbb{C}^d)$. Normalising $z_d = 1$ and
setting $x_j = z_j/z_{j+1}$, positivity is read off the chamber series
\[
  F_d(x) \;=\;
  \frac{\prod_{m<l}\bigl(1 - z_m/z_l\bigr)\,\Qd_d}
       {\prod_{m+r\le l}\bigl(1 - z_m/z_l - z_r/z_l\bigr)}
  \;=\; \frac{N_d(x)}{\prod_{r}\bigl(1 - f_r(x)\bigr)}
  \;=\; \sum_{\beta \ge 0} A_\beta x^\beta ,
\]
where every $f_r$ has nonnegative coefficients and zero constant term. Write
$\tau(i,j,k,\dots) = (j-i,j,k,\dots)$ for the first adjacent transposition, and $C(M)$ for the
$\ell$-free Chern coefficient of [R, Thm.~5].

\section{Infrastructure}

\subsection{Computing \texorpdfstring{$\Qd_d$}{Q\_d}}

We take the route of [N, \S7.3]: rather than parametrise the orbit and eliminate the group
variables, we write down the quadratic relations of Proposition~7.3 directly, saturate by the
defect-zero coordinates to remove the spurious components that appear from $d = 6$ on
([N, \S7.4]), and read the multidegree off an initial ideal.

The design point worth stating is that every quantity the computation can be checked against is
known in advance, and all of them are checked: multihomogeneity of each relation under the torus
weights; the codimension $\dim \Nhat_d - \binom{d}{2}$ forced by homogeneity of the residue
formula; vanishing on random $B_d$-translates of $\eref$; $\deg \Qd_d$; exactness of the chamber
division; and the normalisation fixing the $c_1^d$ coefficient. The stage refuses to emit an
artifact otherwise.  Externally, the resulting Thom polynomials agree with Rim\'anyi's published
tables at every order and relative dimension those cover (\S\ref{sec:a7}).

\subsection{Reaching \texorpdfstring{$d = 7$}{d = 7}}
\label{sec:scaling}

Three observations bring $d = 7$ into range; the arithmetic they save is recorded in the source
rather than here.

\emph{Saturation is a division.} For a homogeneous ideal and degrevlex with $v$ last, a Gr\"obner
basis saturates by $v$ on dividing each element by the largest power of $v$ dividing it. This
replaces the elimination that a general-purpose saturation performs --- and which does not
terminate here --- with one basis computation per defect-zero coordinate. Both the placement of
those coordinates and the sequence in which they are moved to the end matter by two orders of
magnitude, for the same ideal and the same answer.

\emph{Every one of those saturations is needed.} If $\mathrm{in}(I) : v = \mathrm{in}(I)$ then
$I : v = I$, since for $f \in I : v$ we have $v \cdot \mathrm{in}(f) = \mathrm{in}(vf) \in
\mathrm{in}(I)$, and $I \subseteq I : v$ then forces the Hilbert functions equal. The left-hand
condition is combinatorial, so it can be tested on the initial ideal alone after each step. It
never holds until the last coordinate is saturated.

\emph{The two phases after the basis are combinatorics, not algebra.} The initial ideal is
monomial, and $P_S = (x_i : i \in S)$ contains a monomial ideal exactly when $S$ meets the support
of every generator; the minimal primes are therefore the minimal \emph{transversals} of those
supports. Localising at one is a restriction of exponent vectors, and the multiplicity is the
number of monomials outside the resulting artinian ideal. Neither step needs a primary
decomposition, and bounding the transversal search at the expected codimension is sound, since
every subset of a transversal of that size is itself of that size --- which leaves the codimension
check intact in both directions.

One consequence is worth stating, because it is easy to mistake for a discrepancy. The variable
order changes the degeneration but not what it computes: at $d = 7$ two orders give $621$ and $572$
components, with different multiplicities, summing to the same $\Qd_7$. The component structure is
an artefact of the term order; the multidegree is an invariant of the variety. A related null
result: over $\mathbb{Q}$, $\mathrm{GF}(32003)$ and $\mathrm{GF}(2^{31}-1)$ the initial ideals are
identical and the cost is the same to within a few percent, so coefficient growth is not what this
computation is spending on.

\subsection{How much cancellation is being asked for}

Strong Laurent positivity fails from $d = 5$, so some $A_\beta$ are negative; the weak conjecture
survives because each Chern coefficient sums many of them. Table~\ref{tab:cancel} measures the gap.

\input{tables/cancellation}

Three things stand out. The negative coefficients are \emph{rare}, growing from $1\%$ to $2\%$ of
the terms. They are \emph{flat}: every one is $-1$, with a single $-2$ at $d = 7$, while the
positive coefficients reach $10^5$ and beyond. And the mass they carry is a fraction of a percent
of the positive mass. Meanwhile $\min C(M) = 1$ at every order and every relative dimension we
reach --- the Chern coefficients are not merely nonnegative but strictly positive, and never
larger than they need to be at the bottom.

This is the shape of the open question in \S\ref{sec:assessment}. A bound on the negative part of
$C(M)$ in terms of its largest term would settle the conjecture without any pairing, and the
numbers say such a bound has a great deal of room in it.

\subsection{Exactness}

No result below depends on floating point. One point deserves emphasis because it is a trap:
Laurent coefficients are accumulated in \texttt{int64}, and they outgrow it. A wrapped
accumulator reads as a large \emph{negative} number, that is, as a spurious counterexample to
the very conjecture under test. The evaluator therefore replays the fixed point in floating
point as a magnitude probe and raises rather than returning wrapped values.  Beyond that point
the expansion is run in residue arithmetic instead; see \S3.

\section{Results for \texorpdfstring{$A_6$}{A\_6}}

\begin{proposition}[verified]
Rim\'anyi's conjecture holds for $A_6$ at every relative dimension $\ell \le 7$, and for $A_5$ at
$\ell \le 11$.
\end{proposition}

These are finite exact computations, not proofs of the infinite statement. For $A_5$ this matches
the range of [R, Thm.~8]; the $A_6$ verification is new. Past $\ell = 5$ the coefficients outgrow
\texttt{int64} --- they reach $1.75 \times 10^{22}$ at $A_6$, $\ell = 7$ --- so the expansion is
run modulo several word-sized primes and reconstructed by CRT, with one prime held back and spent
on verifying the reconstruction. The grouping into Chern monomials is computed from the grid
geometry rather than from which cells are nonzero, so that the residues of a given coefficient
correspond across primes; grouping by value would misalign them, since a nonzero coefficient can
vanish modulo one prime and not another.

\subsection{External agreement}

one, computed by the restriction-equation method. All $15$, $30$ and $58$ coefficients for $A_4$,

here, this constrains $\Qd_d$ externally rather than self-consistently, and is a considerably
sharper check than the eleven classical values at $\ell = 0$.

\subsection{The reductions of [H] at \texorpdfstring{$d = 6$}{d = 6}}

\begin{observation}
Both reductions of [H] survive at $d = 6$ throughout the tested range ($\deg \le 11$): the
unpaired tail $i > j \Rightarrow A_{i,j,\dots} \ge 0$, and the paired inequality
$A_{i,j,\dots} + A_{j-i,j,\dots} \ge 0$ for $0 \le i \le j$.
\end{observation}

This is evidence that the $\tau$-pairing frame is structural rather than an $A_5$ accident.

\subsection{Prefix positivity fails at \texorpdfstring{$d = 6$}{d = 6}}
\label{sec:prefix}

[H, \S8] observes that every tested coefficient of $B = F_5/(1-a)$ is nonnegative, and
[H, \S11.4] proposes proving that as a stepping stone, since
$B_{i,j,k,l} = \sum_{r \le i} A_{r,j,k,l}$ turns the paired inequality into a statement about
adjacent differences of a prefix array.

\begin{proposition}
$F_6/(1-a)$ is not coefficientwise nonnegative. Indeed $A_{(0,2,3,2,2)} = -1$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The prefix sum runs over $r \le i$, so $B_{0,j,k,l} = A_{0,j,k,l}$ and any negative coefficient
with $i = 0$ survives untouched. $F_5$ has no negative coefficient with $i = 0$ in the tested
range; $F_6$ has two, the first being $A_{(0,2,3,2,2)} = -1$. Confirmed along two independent
code paths, truncated series products in exact integers and the XLA fixed-point grid.
\end{proof}

The route of [H, \S11.4] is therefore specific to $d = 5$. Note that the obstruction is at the
very first level of the prefix sum, so no refinement of the argument recovers it.

\section{\texorpdfstring{$A_7$}{A\_7}: the reductions break, the conjecture does not}
\label{sec:a7}

$\Qd_7$ is computable --- see \S\ref{sec:scaling} --- and it changes the picture.  It is worth
being precise about what fails, because the two are easy to conflate.  Strong Laurent positivity
has been false since $d = 5$ and is no more false at $d = 7$.  Rim\'anyi's conjecture, the actual
target, \emph{survives}: every Chern coefficient of $\Tp_{A_7}$ is positive at every relative
dimension we reach.  What breaks is the \emph{reduction machinery} of [H] --- the two inequalities
through which the $A_5$ programme runs.

\subsection{Validation first}

Because the claim is a negative one, the input has to be beyond question.  Our $\Tp_{A_7}$
reproduces Rim\'anyi's published tables exactly: all $15$ coefficients at $\ell = 0$ and all $105$
at $\ell = 1$, and likewise for $A_4$, $A_5$, $A_6$.  Those tables are maintained by Rim\'anyi
himself at \texttt{tpp.web.unc.edu} and are computed by the restriction-equation method --- an
approach independent of the residue formula implemented here --- so the agreement constrains
$\Qd_7$ externally rather than self-consistently.

We note the limit of that check.  The companion page \texttt{tpp.web.unc.edu/tp-linfinity/}
tabulates the rational functions $F_Q$, whose numerators are the closest published analogue of our
$\Qd_d$; for $A_4$ it gives numerator $2z_1 + z_2 - z_4$, exactly our $\Qd_4$.  But that page stops
at $\mu = 6$: \textbf{no $A_7$ data appears there}, so $\Qd_7$ itself has no published counterpart
to compare against.  The Thom polynomial tables are therefore the strongest external constraint
available, and they are what we use.

Internally, every offending coefficient below agrees between two unrelated code paths --- the
truncated series in exact Python integers and the XLA fixed-point grid --- and all of them sit at
total degree $7$ to $10$, inside the truncation degree, so they are exact rather than artefacts of
a cut-off.

\subsection{How the reductions fail}

\begin{observation}
The unpaired tail fails at $d = 7$: $A_{(2,1,1,2,2,1)} = -1$ with $i = 2 > j = 1$.  Proposition~3
of [H] proves this cannot happen at $d = 5$, and it does not happen at $d = 6$.
\end{observation}

\begin{observation}
The paired inequality fails at $d = 7$, and does so in the sharpest possible way.  Take
$\beta = (1,2,1,2,2,1)$.  Then $j = 2i$, so $\tau(\beta) = \beta$: this is a \emph{fixed point} of
the transposition, where the paired inequality reads $2A_\beta \ge 0$.  But $A_\beta = -1$.  There
is no partner to appeal to.
\end{observation}

$\alpha$ coordinates $\beta = (1,2,1,2,2,1)$ is $\alpha = (1,1,-1,1,0,-1,-1)$, so it contributes to
the Chern monomial indexed by the multiset $M = \{1,1,1,0,-1,-1,-1\}$ --- at $\ell = 0$, the
monomial $c_1 c_2^3$.  That multiset has $35$ ballot orderings, of which eight carry a nonzero
coefficient:

So $d = 7$ separates two things that $d \le 6$ kept together.  The conjecture is about $C(M)$, and
$C(M)$ is fine.  The reductions are about the individual $A_\beta$ and their $\tau$-orbits, and
those are not.  The $\tau$-pairing is a way of certifying the sum by controlling its summands two
at a time; at $d = 7$ the summands are no longer controllable that way, while the sum is unharmed.

\subsection{Consequences}

The $\tau$-pairing frame is therefore $d \le 6$, not structural.  Anything built on it --- the
unpaired-tail reduction, the paired series $\mathcal{P}$ of [H, eq.~(12)], the $J_d(1/2,\cdot)$
analysis of \S6 --- is an argument about small $d$ and cannot be the shape of a general proof.
That is a strong constraint on where to look next, and it is the main reason we report the failure
rather than the verification.

\section{Denominator certificates}
\label{sec:certificates}

\begin{definition}
Let $N / \prod_{r=1}^{R}(1 - f_r)$ with each $f_r$ nonnegative and of zero constant term. An
\emph{order-$k$ certificate} is an identity
\[
  N \;=\; \sum_{|S| \le k} P_S \prod_{r \in S}(1 - f_r),
  \qquad \text{every } P_S \text{ coefficientwise nonnegative,}
\]
over subsets $S \subseteq \{1, \dots, R\}$.
\end{definition}

Dividing through, $F = \sum_S P_S / \prod_{r \notin S}(1 - f_r)$ is a sum of products of
nonnegative series, so $F \ge 0$. Order $0$ is the statement that $N$ itself is nonnegative.
[H, \S10.3] reports a first-order search of this shape coming back infeasible.

\subsection{Why a returned certificate is a proof}

$P_\emptyset$ is not a free unknown: since $\prod_{r \in \emptyset}(1 - f_r) = 1$, it is the
remainder $N - \sum_{S \ne \emptyset} P_S \prod_{r \in S}(1 - f_r)$. We therefore solve a
feasibility LP in the nonempty parts only, snap the solver's floating-point answer to exact
rationals, and recompute $P_\emptyset$ in exact arithmetic. The identity then holds by
construction, and the single remaining question, whether that exact remainder is coefficientwise
nonnegative, is decided exactly. The LP is demoted to a search heuristic; correctness never
depends on it. The output is a finite algebraic identity checkable in any computer algebra
system.

\subsection{Why a failure is a theorem}

\begin{lemma}[projection]
A constraint on a monomial of total degree $T$ involves only the coefficients of the $P_S$ of
degree $\le T$, because every $\prod_{r \in S}(1 - f_r)$ has constant term $1$ and no
negative-degree terms. Truncating both the constraints and the unknowns at degree $T$ is
therefore an exact \emph{projection} of the feasible set, not a further restriction.
\end{lemma}

Consequently infeasibility of the truncated program proves that no certificate of that order
exists at \emph{any} degree. Failed searches become impossibility results.

\subsection{\texorpdfstring{$A_4$}{A\_4}}

\begin{proposition}
$F_4$ admits an order-$4$ certificate with $\deg P_S \le 8$, exhibited explicitly and verified
exactly; and no certificate of order $\le 3$ exists at any degree. The minimal order is $4$.
\end{proposition}

This is an independent computational proof of [N, Thm.~1] by a method using no special feature
of $d = 4$. The lower bound already follows by hand at depth~$1$: writing $m_S$ for the constant
term of $P_S$, matching the constant term of $N = 1 - a - b - c + \cdots$ forces
$\sum_S m_S \le 1$, while matching the coefficients of $a$, $b$, $c$ forces
\[
  \sum_{S \ni f_0} m_S \ge \tfrac12, \qquad
  \sum_{S \ni f_2} m_S \ge 1, \qquad
  \sum_{S \ni f_5} m_S \ge 1,
\]
where $f_0 = 2a$, $f_2 = b + ab$, $f_5 = c + abc$ are the only factors containing the respective
bare monomials. With a budget of $1$ these can only be met if a single $S$ contains all three.
The machine-found certificate saturates all four bounds exactly, its only two constant terms
being $\tfrac12(1-f_0)(1-f_2)(1-f_4)(1-f_5)$ and $\tfrac12(1-f_2)(1-f_4)(1-f_5)(1-f_6)$.

\subsection{\texorpdfstring{$A_5$}{A\_5}: how far the obstruction reaches}

\begin{proposition}
No denominator certificate of order $\le 7$ exists at any degree for $F_5$, nor for
$F_5/(1-a)$.
\end{proposition}

For $F_5$ this is expected, since $F_5 \not\ge 0$, and serves as a control that the engine never
manufactures a certificate for a false statement. For the prefix series, which \emph{is}
coefficientwise nonnegative as far as we compute, it is informative: it extends [H, \S10.3] from
order~$1$ to order~$7$. The bound sharpens with probe depth: depth~$1$ gives $\ge 4$, depth~$2$
gives $\ge 7$, depth~$3$ rules out everything up to $7$.

\section{The unpaired tail}

\subsection{One series}

Set $H(a) = (1-a)/(1-2a)$ and $J_d = F_d / H$. After $H$ is removed, every occurrence of $a$ in
a numerator or denominator monomial is accompanied by $b$: the chamber monomials containing $a$
are $z_1/z_l = ab\cdots$, and only $z_1/z_2 = a$ is bare, which is exactly the factor $1 - 2a$
that $H$ removes. Hence $\deg_a [b^j c^k \cdots] J_d \le j$, and convolving with
$H(a) = 1 + \sum_{n \ge 1} 2^{n-1} a^n$ gives, for $i > j$,
\[
  A_{i,j,k,\dots} \;=\; 2^{\,i-1}\, [b^j c^k \cdots]\, J_d(1/2, b, c, \dots).
\]
The entire unpaired tail, infinitely many coefficients, is therefore one positivity question
about a single series in $d - 2$ variables. This is [H, Lemma~2 and Prop.~3] for $d = 5$, stated
so that it applies verbatim for any $d$.

Our $J_5(1/2,b,c,e)$ agrees with [H, eq.~(9)] coefficient for coefficient to degree $12$, which
is what validates the construction. $J_6(1/2,b,c,d,e)$ is coefficientwise nonnegative as far as
we compute ($\deg \le 10$).

\subsection{Multiplicative certificates}

The two successful proofs in the literature, [N, Thm.~1] and [H, Prop.~3], are
\emph{multiplicative}: products of ratios $(1-u)/(1-v)$, each nonnegative by Lemma~1 because
$v - u \ge 0$. That shape is not a finite sum $\sum_S P_S \prod_{r \in S}(1 - f_r)$ with
$P_S \ge 0$, so the additive certificates of \S4 structurally cannot find them.

The additive search is obstructed on $J_5(1/2,\cdot)$, whose nonnegativity is Proposition~3, so
the additive class is not the one to use here.

Presenting the numerator in factored form, the Vandermonde part being already a factorisation,
one can search for a matching of numerator factors to denominators satisfying the Lemma~1
condition, cancel whatever divides exactly, and ask whether what remains is nonnegative.

\begin{proposition}
For $d = 5$ this closes: all nine numerator factors pair off, three denominators cancel exactly,
and the leftover numerator is exactly $1$. Proposition~3 is reconstructed mechanically.
\end{proposition}

For $d = 6$ the same search does not close, and \S\ref{sec:a7} says why that is not worth
pursuing: the unpaired tail is a statement about individual $A_\beta$, and at $d = 7$ it is false.
The mechanisation is therefore a way of reproducing and checking the small-$d$ arguments of [H],
not a route to a general one.

\section{Gauge freedom in the numerator}
\label{sec:gauge}

Everything above holds $\Qd_d$ fixed. It need not be. This section records what changes when
it is not, following [S] --- whose results we use but have not refereed.

\subsection{The universal kernel}

$\Qd_d$ is not the only numerator computing $\Tp_{A_d}$. Writing $\epsilon_+$ for the map
sending a coefficient array to its distinct-permutation orbit sums, [S, Prop.~3.3] shows that
replacing $\Qd_d$ by $\Qd_d + P$ changes no Thom polynomial at any level exactly when
$\epsilon_+ E_C(\Delta_d P / D_d) = 0$, and that at the array level
$\ker \epsilon_+ = \sum_{i} (1 - s_i)\mathcal{L}_0$. So the two positivity statements sit on
opposite sides of a genuine freedom:
\[
  A_\beta \ \text{depends on the numerator}, \qquad C(M) \ \text{does not}.
\]
A negative $A_\beta$ may therefore be an artefact of the representative. This reframes
\S\ref{sec:certificates}: every obstruction proved there is a statement about the canonical
$\Qd_d$, not about $\Tp_{A_d}$.

\subsection{What this buys at \texorpdfstring{$d = 5$}{d = 5}}

[S, \S4.4--4.5] exploits it. With the linear forms of \S4.3 there,
$\Qd_5 = G(DJ + BC) = GDJ + GBC$; the summand $GBC$ is proved residue-null by two
chamber-safe contour swaps, and the fixed-chamber series of the remaining representative
$GDJ$ has an explicit coefficientwise-positive Stanley decomposition. Strong positivity is
false for $\Qd_5$ but true for $GDJ$, and the weak conjecture follows for $d = 5$ at every
$\ell$ --- with no cancellation argument at all.

We can confirm the consequences against our own $\Qd_5$: the factorisation is exact, our
independently computed quadratic factor is $DJ + BC$ on the nose, the $GDJ$ series has no
negative coefficient to degree $18$ where the canonical one has sixteen, and $GBC$ contributes
zero to every Chern packet in range. The two all-degree lemmas themselves we have not audited.

\subsection{Constructing null kernels from a symmetry}

The mechanism behind the first of those two swaps generalises, and this is our contribution
here. The Chern insertion is symmetric in $z_1, \dots, z_d$ while $\Delta_d$ is antisymmetric,
so if $P/D_d$ is invariant under a transposition $s$ the whole integrand is antisymmetric and
the residue is its own negative. Writing $A_s$ for the product of the factors of $D_d$ that $s$
moves off the factor set:

\begin{proposition}
$P/D_d$ is $s$-invariant if and only if $P = A_s R$ with $R$ an $s$-invariant polynomial.
Subject to the contour being exchangeable, every such $P$ is residue-null at every level.
\end{proposition}

This is degree-free: unlike a search that imposes nullity on the Chern packets fitting under a
truncation, nothing here weakens as $d$ grows. The difference is decisive. At $d = 5$ the
packet route is overdetermined ($48$ packets against $34$ free monomials) and its answer
survives out-of-sample; at $d = 6$ there are $29$ packets against $766$ monomials, the returned
kernel is fitted to the packets it can see, and it breaks five of twenty-one one truncation
higher. The packet supply grows by about four per degree, so no reachable truncation repairs it.

The construction recovers the known case: at $d = 5$ the transposition $s_{45}$ moves exactly
$z_2 + z_3 - z_5$ and $z_1 + z_4 - z_5$, so $A_{s_{45}} = MC$, and $A_{s_{45}}B$ is the summand
$MBC$ of [S, \S4.4]. Of the kernels it produces, \emph{none} fails the packet test at any order
we can check --- $3$ at $d = 5$, $65$ at $d = 6$, $4167$ at $d = 7$ --- and contour-exchangeable
transpositions exist at every order, including $s_{47}$, $s_{57}$, $s_{67}$ at $d = 7$.

The construction is tight in the direction one would hope: $A_sR$ passes the packet test
\emph{exactly} when $R$ is $s$-invariant, so nothing is being left on the table by insisting
on the symmetry.

\subsection{Searching for a positive representative}
\label{sec:gaugesearch}

Since the chamber series is linear in the numerator, looking for a positive gauge is a
mixed-integer programme: one inequality per chamber monomial, with the kernel coefficients as
unknowns. Two families of columns are pooled. Symmetry kernels are null whatever the
truncation. Free monomials are null only where the Chern packets in range \emph{outnumber}
them and so determine nullity --- at $d = 5$, $48$ packets against $34$ monomials; at $d = 6$,
$29$ against $766$, which is why they are admitted at one order and not the other.

Two implementation points are forced rather than chosen. The programme must be posed in
monomial coordinates: a row-reduced basis of the fitted null space has entries near $8\times
10^{9}$ and its solutions cannot be recovered exactly. And the variables must be integers ---
the continuous relaxation returns a fractional vertex, $21$ nonzero coordinates near $1/2$ at
$d = 5$, that no rounding makes feasible.

Everything the solver proposes is recomputed in exact integer arithmetic, and then re-tested
at a truncation the fit never saw. That last step is not a formality. Nullity and
nonnegativity fail differently: a symmetry kernel stays null out of sample by construction,
whereas its positivity is easily an artefact of where the fit stopped, and only the deeper
check tells them apart.

\begin{proposition}
At $d = 5$ the search returns a numerator whose chamber series has no negative coefficient.
Fitted at degree $16$ and verified at $18$ and $20$, it is exactly $\Qd_5 - GBC = GDJ$: the
representative of [S, \S4.5], recovered from scratch by a search told nothing about it.
\end{proposition}

\subsection{Why one transposition is not enough}

At $d = 6$ this does not close, and the obstruction is the same one that is already visible at
$d = 5$. Restricted to symmetry kernels alone, the $d = 5$ programme is infeasible --- at an
order where a positive gauge is known to exist. The reason is exact: $GBC = MBC + FBC$, only
$MBC$ has $P/D_5$ outright $s_{45}$-invariant, and $FBC$ is not divisible by $A_{s_{45}} = MC$.
[S] reaches it by a second swap --- antisymmetrise, then observe that the completed source
multiset of $T(t) = P(t)M(t)$ is $s_{12}$-invariant. At $d = 5$ the free monomials cover the
gap. At $d = 6$ neither family does, and they fail in exactly complementary ways.

\begin{observation}
Fitted at degree $14$, the symmetry kernels alone give a numerator that is genuinely null out
of sample --- no packet sum moves at degree $16$ or $18$ --- but which still carries $9$ of
$42$, then $26$ of $66$, negative coefficients. The gauge is real and is not positive.
Admitting the $766$ free monomials as well and fitting at degree $16$ inverts the failure:
the result is nearly positive, $1$ of $66$ and then $5$ of $99$ negatives surviving, but $12$
and then $27$ packet sums move, so it is not a gauge at all.
\end{observation}

Positivity and nullity are therefore both within reach at $d = 6$, but not simultaneously,
and the two are traded against one another by the choice of column family. That is a sharp
statement of what is missing: a family large enough to be positive whose nullity does not have
to be fitted.

Mechanising the second swap is therefore the concrete next piece of work, and the blocker is
the construction rather than the computation. [A6, \S13] lifts the $d = 5$ kernel into a
four-parameter family at $d = 6$ by a related source-multiset completion, though [A6, \S13.1]
shows the lifted representative is not itself positive, and [A6, \S14.1] shows the freedom
cuts both ways --- an individual $A_\beta$ is not determined by the Thom class, so no marked
coefficient can be an intrinsic invariant.

\section{Assessment}
\label{sec:assessment}

What the computations suggest about proof strategy:

\begin{enumerate}[itemsep=3pt,topsep=3pt]
\item The $\tau$-pairing reduction is \emph{not} the right frame in general.  Both its
  inequalities survive at $d = 6$ and both fail at $d = 7$ (\S\ref{sec:a7}), while Rim\'anyi's
  conjecture survives, so the machinery is specific to small $d$ even though the statement is not.
  The prefix-positivity stepping stone fails one order earlier still.
\item Additive, Positivstellensatz-shaped certificates are obstructed on \emph{every} target
  tried, including provable ones. On present evidence they are not the tool for this problem,
  and we would not recommend further effort extending [H, \S10.3] in that direction.
  This verdict is now scoped, not withdrawn: those obstructions concern the canonical
  $\Qd_d$, and \S\ref{sec:gauge} shows the numerator is only determined up to a residue-null
  kernel. At $d = 5$ a different representative needs no certificate at all, its series being
  outright nonnegative. The right question is not which certificate fits the canonical series
  but which representative needs the least.
\item Multiplicative certificates are the ones that succeed where anything does: they reproduce
  both published proofs from scratch.  But what they certify --- the individual $A_\beta$ --- is
  exactly what stops being controllable at $d = 7$, so they mechanise the small-$d$ arguments
  rather than generalise them.
\item Since the $\tau$-orbit is not the right unit of cancellation, the open question is what is.
  Table~\ref{tab:a7cancel} is suggestive: the negative mass is small, bounded and spread over few
  orderings, while the positive mass is large and concentrated in one.  A bound on the negative
  part of $C(M)$ in terms of its largest term would settle the conjecture without needing any
  pairing at all, and nothing we have computed argues against one existing.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Open questions and sharp edges}
\label{sec:open}

Points where these computations run past what we can justify from the literature. Each states what
was checked, what it settles, and what it does not. Intended for discussion rather than as claims.

\subsection{What \texorpdfstring{$\Qd_d$}{Q\_d} is an invariant of}

Take the corank-two 2-jet space $\Hom(\mathbb{C}^2, \Sym^2\mathbb{C}^2)$, of dimension 6, and the
class $I_{2,2}$ with local algebra $\mathbb{C}[[x,y]]/(xy,\,x^2+y^2) \cong \mathbb{C}[[x,y]]/(x^2,y^2)$.
Four germs of this one class give three different $B_2 \times B_2$-orbit closures
(Table~\ref{tab:reps}); the two standard presentations of the \emph{same} algebra already disagree.

\begin{table}[ht]
\centering
\caption{Borel orbit closures of four germs of the single class $I_{2,2}$.}
\label{tab:reps}
\begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{@{}LLr@{}}
\toprule
germ & orbit closure & $\codim$ \\
\midrule
$(xy,\; x^2+y^2)$   & $q^u_{02}$                          & 1 \\
$(x^2+xy,\; y^2)$   & $q^u_{02}$                          & 1 \\
$(x^2,\; y^2)$      & $q^u_{02},\; q^u_{11}$              & 2 \\
$(y^2,\; x^2)$      & three quadrics, determinantal       & 2 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\end{table}

The natural repair --- demand the representative be generic against the flags --- fails, for a
structural reason. On this space $GL_2 \times GL_2$ \emph{does} have a dense orbit, so $I_{2,2}$ is
the generic corank-two 2-jet and has codimension $0$; but $B_2 \times B_2$ does not, its generic
orbit being $5$-dimensional in a $6$-dimensional space. The action has modality at least one, the
generic jet lies on a one-parameter family of orbits, and their closures are hypersurfaces whose
equations move with the representative. There is no generic Borel orbit to single out.

This is not a defect in [BS]. Their $\Qd_d$ is not ``the class of the $A_d$ locus''; it is the
equivariant class of \emph{one specific} Borel orbit closure, that of a canonically defined
reference jet, and the residue formula is what converts it into a Thom polynomial. The choice of
$\eref$ is part of the theorem, not a normalisation one is free to vary.

\textbf{Question.} Is there a corank-two analogue of $\eref$, together with a residue formula proved
against it? Does the fibration of [BS, Lemma 7.1] have a corank-two counterpart? Without both halves
a corank-two multidegree is a number with no theorem attached, which is why
\texttt{multidegree/corank2.py} stops there and emits no artifact.

\subsection{The acting group beyond 2-jets}

On 2-jets only the \emph{linear} parts of a source and target diffeomorphism act: for $f$ with no
constant or linear term, $f(\varphi_1 + \varphi_2 + \cdots) = f(\varphi_1) + O(3)$ and
$\psi(f) = \psi_1(f) + O(4)$. From order 3 the higher parts contribute, so the acting group is the
jet group. Every $I_{a,b}$ with $b \ge 3$ lives in the $b$-jet space --- $I_{2,3}$ and $I_{3,3}$ in
dimension 14, $I_{2,4}$ in dimension 24 --- so the whole family past $I_{2,2}$ needs this.

\textbf{Question.} What is the right Borel of the jet group, and does the defect-zero saturation
trick of \S\ref{sec:scaling} have an analogue that isolates the orbit component?

\subsection{Which positivity, in which basis}

Rimányi's conjecture is a statement about the relative \emph{Chern monomial} basis, and it is a
corank-one statement. From Rimányi's registry, at relative dimension $0$,
$\Tp(I_{2,2}) = c_2^2 - c_1c_3 = s_{2,2}$, the Giambelli--Thom--Porteous class of $\Sigma^2$. We
expanded the published $I_{2,2}$, $I_{2,3}$, $I_{2,4}$, $I_{3,3}$ in the Schur basis: every one is
Schur-positive, and every one has mixed signs in Chern monomials. So the $I$ family tests Schur
positivity --- Pragacz--Weber --- and refutes any naive extension of the Chern-monomial statement.

\textbf{Question.} Is Chern-monomial positivity special to Morin singularities, or the shadow of a
statement about a basis interpolating between Chern monomials and Schur polynomials? The $A_d$ Thom
polynomials are positive in both; the $I_{a,b}$ only in one.

\subsection{Why \texorpdfstring{$\Qd_8$}{Q\_8} needs different mathematics}

Between $d = 7$ and $d = 8$ the ambient dimension goes $34 \to 50$, $\deg\Qd_d$ goes $13 \to 22$
and the defect-zero coordinates $12 \to 16$. The obstruction is the very first Gr\"obner basis, and
it is not a constant-factor problem.

The ideal is homogeneous, so a degree-bounded basis is exact up to its bound and walking the bound
prices each degree: for $A_8$ the cost multiplies by between four and eight per degree, reaching
degree $9$ at $4$\,GB. The bound that would be needed is the other half of the estimate. The
saturated basis reaches degree $3$ at $d = 5$, $6$ at $d = 6$ and $14$ at $d = 7$, so $d = 8$
should want somewhere near $25$ --- some $10^{11}$ times the cost of degree $9$, with memory giving
out long before time does. Ten variable orders and two algorithms all fail to finish that first
basis; the base field is irrelevant; and seeding the computation with the already-saturated $\Qd_7$
ideal, legitimate because truncating jets maps $\mathcal{O}_8$ into $\mathcal{O}_7$, does not help.

So the question is not how to speed this up but whether $\Qd_8$ is needed. For Rim\'anyi's
conjecture at $d = 8$ it is not: the registry publishes $\Tp_{A_8}$ and $\Tp_{A_9}$ at relative
dimension $0$, computed by restriction equations rather than by a residue formula, and all $22$ and
$30$ coefficients are positive --- the conjecture is supported two orders beyond where we compute.
What $\Qd_8$ would buy is what Chern coefficients cannot give: the individual $A_\beta$, and so the
ability to ask at $d = 8$ what \S\ref{sec:a7} answers at $d = 7$. That argues for a different route
to $\Qd_d$ --- localisation on a resolution, or the fibration of [N, Lemma 7.1] --- rather than a
faster Gr\"obner basis.
